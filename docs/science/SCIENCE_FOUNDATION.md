# Science Foundation

## What a binaural beat is

A binaural beat is a percept produced when slightly different tones are delivered separately to the two ears. A conventional example is 400 Hz to one ear and 420 Hz to the other; listeners may perceive a fluctuation/beat related to the 20 Hz difference. In a pure binaural stimulus, that 20 Hz component is not an acoustic amplitude-modulation component physically present in either single-ear waveform; it emerges from binaural auditory processing.

The first major binaural convergence occurs in the superior olivary complex in the brainstem. Research also finds beat-frequency-related responses further along the auditory pathway and in cortex. This supports the existence of a central neural response to binaural disparity, but it does **not** by itself prove that the listener's whole-brain EEG becomes locked to the beat or that a particular psychological state follows.

## Perceptual constraints are approximate

A 2023 systematic review summarizes classic psychophysical findings as follows: binaural beats are typically discussed around 1–30 Hz differences; carriers below about 1000 Hz are generally favorable; perception has often been reported strongest around roughly 400 Hz; above roughly 30 Hz difference two tones may increasingly be heard separately. These are approximate and vary across individuals and methods.

Important product implication: use these numbers as guidance and warnings, not magical hard boundaries. Published experiments have tested 34–40 Hz and even higher differences. The editor must permit such settings while marking them as outside/near the edge of conventional perceptual assumptions when appropriate.

## “400 Hz is best” is too strong

Around 400 Hz is an important historical/perceptual reference and is common in experiments. It is not a universal outcome optimum. A 2025 parametric sustained-attention experiment crossed beta/gamma beat frequency, 340/400 Hz carrier, onset timing, and white-noise masking. Effects depended on parameter combinations, and the study's positive behavioral pattern appeared under a 40 Hz gamma condition with a 340 Hz carrier and white-noise context rather than proving 400 Hz universally superior.

The product therefore uses no global “best carrier.” Research presets reproduce their source parameters; user templates may default to a comfortable mid-frequency carrier but explain that it is a starting point, not a proven efficacy optimum.

## Neural entrainment is unsettled

The strongest reason not to repeat old “frequency X makes the brain state X” marketing is the EEG literature itself.

The 2023 PLOS ONE systematic review included 14 qualifying studies. Its headline synthesis was inconsistent: five studies supported the brainwave-entrainment hypothesis, eight were contradictory, and one was mixed; substantial methodological heterogeneity limited comparability. The review concluded that whether binaural beats reliably entrain EEG activity in the assumed manner remains unresolved.

A carefully controlled 2020 eNeuro study comparing binaural and monaural beats reported subcortical responses to carrier frequencies and cortical responses related to beat frequency, plus differences in connectivity. However, monaural beats could produce stronger cortical entrainment in some conditions, and the study did not find the expected mood effects. This is exactly why “measurable auditory response” and “useful behavioral outcome” are separate evidence categories in the app.

## Outcome evidence: promising in places, contradictory overall

A 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies / 35 effect sizes reported an overall medium standardized effect (Hedges' g about 0.45) across cognition, anxiety, and pain outcomes. It also found that exposure timing and duration appeared relevant. This is a meaningful positive signal, but it combines heterogeneous outcomes/protocols and should not be converted into one universal frequency prescription.

There are positive specific studies. A 2020 Scientific Reports study found stronger 40 Hz neural entrainment than 16 Hz and reported a between-session training benefit in an attentional-blink paradigm, although performance did not improve within a session. A 2025 perioperative meta-analysis of 15 randomized trials reported reductions in anxiety and pain, while also reporting very high heterogeneity for anxiety outcomes; the authors explicitly called for standardized protocols and further high-quality trials.

There are also important negative results. A large 2023 home-use experiment involving roughly 1000 participants reported worse fluid-intelligence task scores under binaural-beat conditions rather than improvement. Individual studies and reviews have also reported null or inconsistent EEG and mood effects.

Product implication: never map “alpha = relaxation,” “theta = meditation,” “beta = focus,” etc. into guaranteed effects. EEG band names can be educational metadata only. Goal-oriented presets must say “designed for” or “studied for,” not “causes.”

## Why the percept works without claiming too much

At the engineering level the mechanism is straightforward enough to reproduce: each ear receives a phase-coherent sinusoid at a slightly different frequency. The auditory system is sensitive to interaural timing/phase differences and combines information from the two ears centrally, giving rise to a periodic percept associated with the difference frequency.

At the neuroscience level the resulting response can be measured in parts of the auditory pathway, and some experiments observe frequency-following or steady-state activity related to the beat. Whether that local/evoked response propagates into a broad brain-state change, and whether such a change reliably improves attention, relaxation, sleep, pain, anxiety, memory, or another outcome, depends on protocol and remains incompletely resolved.

That two-layer explanation—**reliable acoustic/perceptual construction, uncertain generalized outcome**—is the science policy for the product.

## Headphones and channel isolation

Classical binaural presentation requires separate frequencies to reach separate ears; studies typically use headphones for this reason. Consumer speaker playback introduces acoustic cross-talk between ears and is not a reliable default. The app therefore recommends stereo headphones/earbuds and includes left/right tests.

A browser cannot know what sound actually reaches the ear. Operating-system spatial audio, mono modes, hearing enhancement, Bluetooth processing, amplifier/headphone frequency response, and hardware resampling can all alter the physical output. Research-grade claims require calibrated hardware and documented playback conditions beyond what an ordinary website can infer.

## What the application may safely teach

It may state that binaural beats are a real psychoacoustic phenomenon; explain the difference-frequency percept; explain conventional carrier/difference constraints as approximate; reproduce exact published stimuli; summarize study outcomes and limitations; and identify EEG band terminology.

It must not state as settled fact that a given beat frequency releases a neurotransmitter, treats a disorder, replaces sleep, raises IQ, induces out-of-body experiences, unlocks chakras, or forces the entire brain into an EEG state. Historic BWGen community presets contain claims of that kind, so imports must retain them only as attributed legacy/community descriptions with an explicit unverified label.

## 2026 theta-frequency evidence update

A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis (Fatima et al., PMID 42349368; DOI 10.1016/j.explore.2026.103471) included 13 randomized controlled trials of theta-frequency binaural beats. Only four trials (173 participants) were poolable for pain, with low-certainty evidence; anxiety and cognition were not pooled because of substantial protocol/outcome heterogeneity. This does not justify a “theta treats pain/anxiety/cognition” rule. The app therefore continues to grade evidence per **specific protocol and outcome**, not per EEG-band label.
